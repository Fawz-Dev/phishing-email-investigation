# 🔐 Phishing Email Investigation

## 📌 Project Overview

This project is a simulated phishing email investigation created for cybersecurity learning and portfolio purposes.

The goal was to examine a suspicious email and identify the signs that could indicate a phishing or social engineering attack.

> **Note:** This is a fictional training scenario. No real Bank of Ghana email address, website, or user information was used.

---

## 📧 Simulated Email

**From:** `security-alert@bank0fghana.example`

**Subject:** `URGENT: Your Bank of Ghana Account Requires Verification`

### Email Message

> Your account has been flagged for unusual activity.
>
> Your account will be suspended unless you complete a quick verification.
>
> Send your Bank ID for quick verification to prevent your account from being suspended.
>
> Please complete the verification immediately.

---

## 🚩 Initial Findings

### 1. Lookalike Spelling

I noticed that the sender uses `bank0fghana` instead of `bankofghana`.

The letter **o** has been replaced with the number **0**.

This is suspicious because the altered spelling can make the address look similar to the name of a legitimate organization.

### 2. Suspicious Domain

The email uses the fictional domain:

`bank0fghana.example`

This is not the legitimate Bank of Ghana domain.

The use of a lookalike name combined with a suspicious domain is an indicator that the email may not be from the oo organization it claims to represent.

### 3. Urgent Request

The email uses an urgent request and tells the recipient to complete the verification immediately.

This type of urgency can pressure someone into acting quickly without taking enough time to verify whether the email is legitimate.

### 4. Social Engineering

The email asks the recipient to send their **Bank ID for quick verification**.

This is a social engineering technique because the message attempts to persuade the recipient to provide potentially sensitive information.

---

## 🧠 Analysis

Based on the indicators I identified, the email shows several characteristics commonly associated with phishing attacks.

The main warning signs are:

- Lookalike spelling using `0` instead of `o`
- Suspicious domain
- Urgent request
- Request for potentially sensitive information
- Social engineering techniques

These indicators make the email suspicious and show how an attacker could attempt to make a phishing message appear legitimate.

---

## 🛡️ Recommended Actions

If I received an email like this in a real situation, I would:

- Avoid sending my Bank ID or other sensitive information.
- Avoid clicking suspicious links.
- Verify the message through the organization's official communication channels.
- Report the suspicious email to the appropriate security or IT team.
- Delete the email after reporting it if it is confirmed to be malicious.
